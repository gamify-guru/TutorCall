Your detective language kit

Grammar & Rules

Open a rule whenever you need it. These exact patterns appear in Agnes’s notes.

01 / too + adjective / adverb

too + adjective / adverb

Use too for more than you want or need. It often shows a problem.

The corridor was too dark to read the tag.
The ink was too faint to read.

02 / adjective / adverb + enough

adjective / adverb + enough

Enough comes after an adjective or adverb. It means sufficient.

The handwriting was neat enough.
Voss did not write clearly enough.

03 / enough + noun

enough + noun

Before a noun, enough tells us the amount is sufficient.

Abernathy had enough time to reach the storage room.
We have enough clues for a new lead.

04 / Word order check

too small · small enough · enough time

Do not put enough before an adjective. Do not put too after it.

enough faint → too faint
enough careful → careful enough

Meaning matters, detective. “Too dark” means the darkness caused a problem. “Dark enough” means there was sufficient darkness for a purpose.
